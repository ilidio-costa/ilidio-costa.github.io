**Organization:** Porto Space Team | **Role:** Chemical Department Member | **Timeline:** October 2023 - October 2024

## 1. Introduction

### Context

The [Porto Space Team](https://www.portospaceteam.pt/en) is an association made up of university students, from mainly from FEUP (Faculty of Engineering of the University of Porto) and FCUP (Faculty of Sciences of the University of Porto) with the mission is to produce meaningful research, while teaching aspiring engineers to become even better professionals.

Participated in the Chemical Department of INVICTUS Project, a self pressured hybrid chemical rocket with a estimateed apogee 3 km, which competed at [Euroc24](https://euroc.pt/). The Chemical Department was responsible for the delevopment of the fuel grain, ignition method and ablative liner.

## 2. Technical Contributions & Research

My contributions to the Chemical Department focused on translating theoretical propellant research into flight-ready fuel components for the INVICTUS rocket. This work involved optimizing the chemical properties and manufacturing processes of the fuel grain to ensure the system could withstand the high-pressure environment of a 3 km apogee launch.

Conducted research on paraffin-based grains additives and optimize the geometry with simulation in python to predict performance. Developted the production process of ABS reinforced reinforced fuel grains.

<details>
  <summary><b>Collaborative Engineering</b>:</summary>
  <p>
    Worked closely with the propulsion team to ensure the chemical properties of the fuel were compatible with the mechanical design of the combustion chamber. One of the most significant engineering challenges was the Hot Fire test campaign. This required rigorous logistical planning, as we had to coordinate the transport of sensitive fuel components and testing equipment to a remote site 30 minutes away. Organizing the setup, safety protocols, and execution at the test site taught me the vital importance of clear communication and preparedness in high-stakes field testing.
  </p>

<details>
  <summary><b>Problem Solving & Field Engineering</b>:</summary>
  <p>
    Engineering in a competition environment often requires immediate, creative solutions to unforeseen problems. During the development phase and at Euroc, I actively identified and resolved critical bottlenecks.
    A specialized tool required to open the elastic retaining ring securing the combustion chamber was missing during a critical assembly window. I designed and fabricated an improvised tool using two steel bars to successfully open the ring, allowing the team to proceed with the engine assembly and meet our launch deadline.
  </p>

## 3. Take-aways

- <b>Adaptability in High-Pressure Environments</b>: My experience at EuroC taught me that engineering success often depends on the ability to improvise. Fabricating a custom tool from steel bars to solve a critical assembly bottleneck demonstrated that resourcefulness is just as vital as theoretical knowledge when facing tight competition deadlines.

- <b>Cross-Disciplinary Synergy</b>: Working at the intersection of the Chemical and Propulsion departments showed me that systems integration is a two-way street. Ensuring fuel chemistry aligned perfectly with mechanical chamber design required constant communication to balance performance with structural safety.

- <b>Data-Driven Iteration</b>: Utilizing Python simulations to optimize grain geometry proved that predictive modeling is essential for reducing the number of costly physical prototypes. This allowed us to iterate faster and enter the testing phase with higher confidence in our fuel performance.

## 1. Introduction

### Context

Following my time in the Chemical department, I stepped into a leadership role in the new Propulsion department, the fusion of the previeous Propulsion and Chemical Departments, to oversee the development of the hybrid propulsion motor for INVICTUS II, hybrid chemical propulsion rocket with a target 3 km apogee which contited at [Euroc25](https://euroc.pt/).

## 2. Technical Contributions & Research

Leading the newly unified Propulsion department required a balance between organizational restructuring and technical innovation. My focus was on establishing a culture of data-driven decision-making, where every design iteration was validated through custom analytical models. By standardizing our documentation and recruitment processes, I aimed to bridge the gap between high-level project management and the granular fluid dynamics required for a successful hybrid launch.

## 3. Take-aways

- <b>Organizational Leadership & Change Management</b>: Successfully managed the fusion of two engineering departments, creating a unified workflow that integrated chemical propellant research with mechanical propulsion hardware.

- <b>Merit-Based Team Growth</b>: Established a rigorous recruitment framework using technical case studies, ensuring the department was staffed by high-aptitude engineers capable of meeting Euroc's standards.

- <b>Analytical Validation Framework</b>: Implemented a "simulation-first" approach by developing Python-based tools (utilizing Coolprop at this stage) to validate engine performance before committing to physical testing.

- <b>Fiscal Responsibility in R&D</b>: Gained experience in managing annual budgets and sponsor relations, learning to optimize engineering goals within strict financial and market constraints.

- <b>Technical Communication</b>: Developed the ability to translate advanced fluid dynamics and combustion theory into standardized engineering protocols and team-wide training workshops.
